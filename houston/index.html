When Your System Fails in Houston

Heating and cooling failures rarely wait for a convenient day, which is why we offer fast, flexible scheduling for most situations. When you call, you'll speak with a real person who gets a technician headed to your property quickly. Arrival in the area is usually within a day for urgent calls, and a dead AC in a heat wave or a furnace out on a freezing night is exactly the kind of call we move to the front of the line.

The emergency calls we run tend to follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. Year-round, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, clogged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have quit. Since our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these are wrapped up in one visit, with the price agreed before any work begins.

A little caution while you wait for us can head off a much larger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw safely. Running it frozen risks an overflowing drain pan and a damaged compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us right away.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Houston

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your system. Its age tells you a lot. With regular service, most air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that range and is also showing higher bills, uneven temperatures, or frequent repairs, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install in peak season.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a miserable night.

Comfort Challenges Houston Homeowners Face

Most local HVAC failures announce themselves before they happen. A breaker that trips more than once, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, energy bills creeping up with nothing else changed. Those are early signs of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're an affordable repair. Ignored, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
